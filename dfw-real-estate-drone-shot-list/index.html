1. Capture the essential property views

Front hero angles

Use front three-quarter views from both sides to show the entry, facade, frontage, driveway and property depth.

Rear and exterior features

Show the rear elevation, backyard and verified features such as a pool, outdoor living, acreage or water.

Overhead clarity

Use a straight-down view when it helps explain the lot, roofline or improvements. It does not establish a legal boundary.

Immediate context

Show the home within its surroundings without exaggerating distance, access or the relationship to nearby amenities.

Information to send first

  • Property location and listing-live deadline
  • Top three selling features
  • Access, gate, parking, tenant or pet instructions
  • Views to avoid or seller privacy concerns
  • Requested photo count and delivery sizes
  • Vertical-reel duration and branding needs

Prepare the exterior

  • Move vehicles, bins, hoses and temporary clutter
  • Confirm landscaping and pool areas are presentation-ready
  • Open blinds or turn on exterior lights when appropriate
  • Notify occupants and on-site contacts
  • Identify any neighboring area that should be avoided
  • Keep a weather backup date available

2. Use location context honestly

Golf courses, water, parks, trails, community amenities, major roads and development context can be valuable when they are genuinely relevant. The framing and caption should not imply that an amenity belongs to the property, exaggerate proximity or present an approximate line as a legal boundary.

Planning note

If the listing depends on acreage, easements, lot lines or another legal boundary, obtain the appropriate survey or licensed professional guidance. Aerial media can complement that information but does not replace it.

3. Plan the vertical property reel

Open with the strongest reveal

Use the best exterior angle in the first one to two seconds so the viewer immediately understands the property.

Move from wide to specific

Show the property context, facade, lot and most marketable verified exterior features in a simple sequence.

Design for 9:16

Use short, stable clips framed for mobile, and leave safe space when the agent will add text, music or branding.

End with a clear CTA area

Finish on a clean hero view or a frame that gives the listing team room for contact and showing information.

Real estate drone planning questions

Which aerial photos are most useful for a listing?

A useful set usually includes front and rear three-quarter views, a wide property-context image, an overhead view when it clarifies the lot or roofline, and lower aerials of verified exterior features.

What should a DFW agent send before the shoot?

Send the property location, listing-live deadline, top selling features, access instructions, views to avoid, requested photo count and whether the campaign needs a vertical reel.

Can aerial listing images show nearby amenities?

Yes, when the amenity is genuinely relevant, access is permitted and the media does not exaggerate distance or imply a false relationship to the property.

Does a drone image establish property boundaries?

No. Drone imagery provides visual and marketing context and does not replace a licensed survey or establish legal property boundaries.
